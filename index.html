Case study

The estimating system at One Day Doors. I owned it from first sketch to rollout.

From tape measure to signed sale

Design, build & rollout · 2025–2026 · Laravel, React, TypeScript

Field crews measured doorways on paper, and salespeople rebuilt the quote by hand later. I replaced both with one system that runs on a tablet in the customer's home, from the first measurement to the signature.

  1. 01Site survey
  2. 02Opening builder
  3. 03Pricing engine
  4. 04Estimate versions
  5. 05Presentation
  6. 06Signature & sale

Measurements that hold up

Each opening is checked as it's entered: whether the door is available in that size, what it will cost, and whether it needs shop work. Door-specific math like double-swing slab width with the astragal reveal is done for the crew, so problems surface while they're still at the house, not once the order reaches the factory.

Pricing with every real rule

Dealer multipliers and markups, buy-one-get-one priced per slab, shop charges, discounts spread across openings, and sales tax by showroom or zip code. When a special-order door has no list price, the engine prices it from the closest comparable door.

Built to close

Salespeople branch an estimate into versions, present it with a pitch sheet, then capture an e-signature and payment. Finalizing creates the sale record and balance due, so nothing is re-keyed.

I designed the flow in Figma prototypes and wrote the documentation before building it, then shipped it with training videos, in-app product tours and release notes. It's used by field crews at 87 locations, and I later migrated the legacy site surveys into the new estimate model so every past job lives in one place.
